## Introduction to Continual Learning

**Continual learning represents a paradigm shift in the field of artificial intelligence, moving the objective from achieving high performance on a static benchmark to building adaptive intelligence that can thrive in a world of constant change.**

In recent years, research has focused on developing neural networks that can learn continuously without experiencing catastrophic forgetting, a phenomenon where the network forgets previously learned information when new information is introduced. This challenge is particularly pronounced in large language models (LLMs), which have a limited capacity to adapt to new data without forgetting previously learned knowledge.

To address this issue, researchers have proposed various approaches, including the Nested Learning paradigm, which offers a robust foundation for closing the gap between the limited learning abilities of current LLMs and the remarkable continual learning abilities of the human brain.

Some key challenges in continual learning include:
• Catastrophic forgetting: the loss of previously learned information when new information is introduced
• Stability-plasticity dilemma: the trade-off between maintaining stability in the network and allowing it to adapt to new information
• Data efficiency: the ability to learn from a limited amount of data without forgetting previously learned information
